Husk leaf blades surrounding the ear directly influence grain filling, making husk leaf architecture critical for yield. We identify Husk Extended Leaf Blade 1 (HELD1) as a major regulator of husk leaf blade extension. A transposon insertion in HELD1 recruits the transcription factor TEOSINTE BRANCHED 1 (TB1) to upregulate tru1 expression, repressing husk leaf blade extension during domestication. HELD1 functions within a broader regulatory network that positions the ligule–auricle boundary and regulates leaf blade–husk proportions. The HELD1 locus experienced significant human selection during maize domestication, whereas reintroduction of the HELD1 allele from the wild progenitor significantly enhances yields in maize. These findings reveal how transposons with regulatory elements modify husk leaf architecture during domestication. Husk leaves are the closest photosynthetic organs to the ear, which contribute directly to grain filling and have potential for yield improvement in maize. Teosinte has a long husk extended leaf blade, whereas maize exhibits a reduced or even no husk leaf blade. However, the genetic basis of this transition in the husk leaf during domesti cation remains unknown. In this study, we identified a major quantitative trait locus (QTL), Husk Extended Leaf Blade 1 (HELD1), corresponding to the tassels replace upper ears1 (tru1) gene. A 133-base pair (bp) Harbinger transposon inserted into the tru1 intron introduces two TEOSINTE BRANCHED 1 (TB1) binding motifs, thereby recruiting TB1 to directly enhance tru1 transcription. Further analyses revealed that TRU1 interacts with the transcription factor ALOG1, which in turn enhances the expression of liguleless2 (lg2), a key regulator of ligule development. Together with brassinosteroid-deficient dwarf1 (brd1) and GA2ox1, these genes set up a regulatory net work that defines the ligular-auricular position, controls the leaf blade-to-husk (sheath) ratio in husk and stem leaves, and regulates husk leaf blade length. Population genetic analyses indicate that HELD1 experienced significant human selection, resulting in the transposon insertion becoming the dominant allele in domesticated maize. Additionally, the teosinte HELD1 allele with extended husk leaf blades significantly enhances yield in maize. Our results demonstrate HELD1 is an important regulator of husk and stem leaf architectures, contributing to the ligular-auricular position to adjust the ratio of leaf blade to husk or sheath.

Leaves, serving as key organs for photosynthesis in plants, supply the carbohydrates that contribute to yield in cereals. Maize develops two major types of leaves: stem leaves and husk leaves. Stem leaves come from the shoot apical meristem (SAM) (1). In contrast, husk leaves arise from lateral meristems that develop into short branches, which initiate husk leaves and give rise to the female inflorescence (2). The adult stem leaf comprises three components: the proximal sheath, the middle auricular–ligular region, and the distal leaf blade (1). Similarly, the husk leaf contains a proximal husk, a middle auricular-ligular region, and an extended distal leaf blade (Fig. 1). The stem leaf sheath anchors the leaf to the stalk (3), which in turn helps reinforce the stalk. The stem leaf blade primarily supplies carbohydrates that support maize yield. In contrast, the husk surrounding the ear functions as a shelter, and protects it from pests such as insects and birds, as well as environmental stresses such as heat (4). The number, width, and length of husks are associated with kernel dehydration in maize, significantly affecting final kernel moisture content (5). However, it remains unclear whether the extended husk leaf blade provides carbohydrates to the ear, similar to the role of the stem leaf blade. Interestingly, removal of husk leaf blades reduces grain yield in some maize lines (6, 7), and 13C feeding revealed that husk leaves, as the closest photosynthetic organs to ear, had higher photosynthetic efficiency than all the stem leaves (8). These facts highlight the potential of husk leaves for the yield improvement in maize. When comparing maize to its wild progenitor, teosinte, one generally neglected and striking difference is the husk leaf blade extension. Teosinte typically develops much longer husk leaves that extend over the husk (Fig. 1A), whereas domesticated maize, except for sweet corn and popcorn, generally has shorter husk leaf blades or none at all (Fig. 1B). The genetic basis underlying this transition in husk leaf development during maize domes tication remains unknown. Results

HELD1 Is a Major QTL Responsible for Husk Leaf Blade Extension in Maize. To identify the molecular genetic basis of husk leaf blade extension in maize, we conducted QTL mapping using a BC2S3 mapping population generated from a cross between the teosinte accession CIMMYT8759 and the maize inbred line W22 (13). Teosinte generally grows long husk leaf blades. In contrast, W22 produces very short and nearly absent husk leaf blade (Fig. 1C). Genome-wide QTL mapping identified 12 QTLs for husk leaf blade extension located on chromosomes 1, 2, 3, 4, 6, 7, and 8 (Fig. 2A and SI Appendix, Fig. S1 A and B). These QTLs accounted for 28.2% of the total phenotypic variation in husk leaf extension. Among these identified QTLs, the one on the long arm of chromosome 3 with the largest genetic effect was designated as HELD1 (Fig. 2A and SI Appendix, Fig. S1A). To precisely quantify the genetic effect of HELD1, a pair of near-isogenic lines (NILs) was then generated (Fig. 1C). The NILs (BC2S6) carried a heterozygous chromosomal fragment at HELD1, while being homozygous at most other loci. NIL-Teo, carrying the teosinte HELD1 allele, exhibited longer husk leaves, a higher ratio of husk leaf blade length to husk length, and a higher ratio of main-stem leaf blade length to sheath length compared with NIL-W22 carrying the W22 HELD1 allele (Fig. 1 I–K). A detailed examination showed that the husk leaf contains a ligular–auricular region between the husk and the extending leaf blade in NIL-Teo (Fig. 1 D and E). However, the ligular-auricular region does develop but remains clearly narrow at the tip of the husk in NIL-W22 (Fig. 1 D and F). Longer extending leaf blades at the tip of the husk are generally correlated with larger and clearer ligular-auricular regions (Fig. 1H), suggesting that development of the ligular-auricular region is strongly correlated with husk leaf blade length. This structure of the husk leaf in NIL-Teo is similar to that of a leaf emerging on the stem, where the husk corresponds to the sheath of the stem leaf (Fig. 1G).

Fine-Mapping of HELD1. To fine-map the HELD1 locus in maize, a large population of 15,500 individuals was derived from 15 residual heterozygous lines (BC2S4), which harbored a heterozygous genotype at the HELD1 locus while homozygous at most other loci. A total of ten simple sequence repeat (SSR) and insertion/deletion (Indel) markers, and one Kompetitive Allele Specific PCR (KASP) marker were developed, and a modified progeny-testing strategy was used for fine-mapping (Materials and Methods and SI Appendix, Fig. S2). The HELD1 locus was finally narrowed to an 11.5-kilobase (kb) genomic segment flanked by the markers M7 and M10, spanning 152,083,622 to 152,095,127 bp on chromosome 3, based on the B73 genome sequence (V4; http://www.maizegdb.org) (Fig.  2 A and B and SI  Appendix, Fig. S2). Only one gene, Zm00001d042111, was annotated in this fine-mapped region (Fig. 2B). Zm00001d042111, corresponding to tassel replacing the upper ear 1 (tru1) (14), regulates axillary branching in maize. tru1 contains two exons and one intron (Fig. 2B), encoding an ankyrin repeat domain protein that contains a BTB/POZ domain with 508 amino acid residues (14). Sequence comparison of tru1 between the two parents, teosinte and W22, revealed 26 single nucleotide polymorphisms (SNPs) and eight Indel variants in the coding and intron regions (SI Appendix, Fig. S3). A 133-bp insertion, as the largest Indel among these variants, was present in the intron of W22. Sequence analysis of this 133-bp insertion using RepeatMasking (https://www.girinst.org/censor/index.php) revealed that it belongs to a Harbinger transposon in maize (SI Appendix, Fig. S4).

tru1, Corresponding to HELD1, Determines the Ratio of Leaf Blade to Husk or Sheath in Maize. To determine whether the tru1 gene (Zm00001d042111), corresponding to HELD1, controls husk leaf extension in maize, we compared the phenotype of the tru1-ws mutant and its wild-type control, A619, at two locations: Beijing and Hainan (Fig. 2C and SI Appendix, Fig. S5 A–C). The tru1-ws mutant exhibited significantly longer husk leaf blades and a greater husk leaf blade-to-husk ratio but no change in the total husk leaf length (Fig. 2 C–E and SI Appendix, Fig. S5 B and C), compared to A619. These results indicate that tru1, corresponding to HELD1, functions to inhibit distal husk leaf blade growth and promote proximal husk growth. We also measured the leaf blade length and sheath length of the first leaf above the first ear on the stem in the tru1-ws mutant and A619. Compared to A619, the tru1-ws mutant plants exhibited longer distal leaf blades, significantly shortened proximal sheaths, and a greater blade-to-sheath ratio, with no change in total leaf length (Fig. 2 F and G), consistent with the expression of TRU1 protein in husk and preligule region, stem leaf sheath, and preligule through immunolocalization (14). These results collectively suggest that tru1, as a key regulator of the proximal–distal leaf pattern, not only controls the ratio of husk leaf blade to husk, but also affects the ratio of stem leaf blade to sheath.

TB1 Directly Binds to the Key Site of HELD1 to Enhance Its Expression Levels, Thereby Repressing the Husk Leaf Blade Extension during Maize Domestication. To identify which variant in the fine-mapping region is responsible for husk leaf blade extension, we performed association mapping. Association mapping of husk leaf extension in a population containing 226 maize inbred lines revealed that the strongest signal was present at the site of the 133-bp TE insertion in the 11.5-kb fine-mapping region (SI  Appendix, Fig.  S6A), indicating that the 133-bp insertion may be responsible for husk leaf blade repression. Expression analysis revealed that tru1 had significantly higher expression levels in the small ears at the stages of V9, V11, and V15 and in the husk leaf ligule region at the stages of V13 and V16 in NIL-W22 compared with NIL-Teo (Fig. 3A). The 133-bp transposable insertion sequence in the intron of W22 contains two TB1 binding motifs (AGGGCT) at both ends (Fig. 3B) (14). This 133-bp sequence can form a hairpin loop (SI Appendix, Fig. S6B) (14). TB1 has been shown to bind to this hairpin loop in the previous study (14). Luciferase transient assays revealed that the effector overexpressing tb1 significantly enhanced luciferase activity under the control of the mini 35S promoter fused with the W22 intron carrying the 133- bp sequence, compared with constructs containing a mutated TB1-binding site at both ends or lacking the 133-bp sequence (Fig. 3 C and D). Similarly, the same effector also enhanced the luciferase activities controlled by the mini 35S promoter fused with the teosinte intron bearing the 133-bp sequence, compared with the corresponding construct lacking the 133-bp sequence (Fig. 3D). Furthermore, the LUC transient demonstrated that the 133-bp insertion upstream of mini 35S promoter in both orientations and downstream of the LUC reporter significantly enhanced the LUC activity, however, the insertion downstream of the reporter produced stronger activity than the insertion placed upstream of mini 35S promoter in both orientations (SI Appendix, Fig. S6C). The Gene Regulatory Network of HELD1 for Husk Leaf Blade Extension in Maize. To understand how tru1 modulates downstream genes involved in the proximal–distal ratio of husk leaves, RNA sequencing (RNA-seq) was performed in the narrow ligular-auricular region of the husk leaf (Fig. 4A), which is vitally important to the proximal–distal ratio, at the V16 stage between NIL-W22 and NIL-Teo. The analysis identified 1,075 upregulated and 837 downregulated differentially expressed genes (DEGs) in the NIL-W22 compared with the NIL-Teo (Dataset  S1). We subsequently conducted a gene ontology (GO) analysis on the DEGs based on RNA-seq (http://systemsbiology.cau.edu.cn/ agriGOv2/). The analysis revealed 104 significantly enriched GO terms (FDR < 0.05) associated with microtubule-based processes, cell movement and cell division, preparing for leaf extension, including plant-type cell wall biogenesis, cell wall biogenesis, cell cycle process, movement of cells or subcellular components, carbohydrate metabolic process, cell cycle, microtubule-based movement, cellulose metabolic process, cell wall macromolecule metabolic process, cellulose catabolic process, mitotic cell cycle process, mitotic cell cycle, cell division, cytoskeleton organization, and cellular hormone metabolic process (SI Appendix, Table S1). These pathways are highly correlated with leaf development.

These results suggest that tru1 plays an important role in leaf development. S everal DEGs were related to husk leaf development, including GA2ox1 for gibberellin (GA) biosynthesis (1, 15), brd1 related to brassinosteroid (BR) biosynthesis (16), and lg2 involved in maize ligule development (17) (Fig. 4A and Dataset S1). lg2 encodes a basic-leucine zipper (bZIP) protein and lg2-R mutant grows ligules and auricles at the both margins of the leaf, lacking blade–sheath boundary over the midrib (18, 19). The GA2ox1 gene represses the GA biosynthesis and is expressed in the developing ligular-auricular region (1, 15). Overexpression of brd1 leads to normal ligule and enlarged auricle (20). TRU1 promotes the expression levels of lg2 and brd1, and r epresses the GA2ox1 tran script. The relative expression levels of all these DEGs were con firmed by real-time qPCR (SI Appendix, Fig. S8 A–C).

To identify whether the lg2 gene regulates maize husk leaf blade extension, we carried out knockout editing targeting the lg2 CDS through CRISPR/Cas9 (SI Appendix, Fig. S9A). Two transgenic events (lg2-CR1, lg2-CR2 ) in the B104 and LH244 genetic back grounds were created. lg2-CR1 and lg2-CR2 display compact plant architecture with narrow stem leaf angles (SI Appendix, Fig. S9 B and C). The HELD1 Locus Was under Selectionduring Maize Diversification. To determine whether the HELD1 locus was under selection in maize, we selected 65 accessions, including nine teosinte and 56 maize accessions, to calculate DNA diversity of a 3.2-kb fragment containing the 133-bp transposon in the tru1 intron (Dataset S3). The DNA diversity of the teosinte accessions was apparently higher than that of the maize lines, and the 133-bp insertion was located within the region of tru1 with relatively low diversity (Fig. 6A). Tajima’s D test significantly rejected the neutral null hypothesis (Tajima’s D = −1.79774, P < 0.05) (Fig.  6A), suggesting that HELD1 underwent selection sweep during maize domestication. We further detected the allele frequency of the 133-bp transposon in 100 teosinte and 242 maize accessions (SI Appendix, Table S2). Most teosinte samples did not contain this 133-bp insertion, with the exception of three homozygous and 16 heterozygous accessions at the HELD1 locus (Fig. 6B), which account for 19% of all teosinte samples. In contrast, the majority of maize accessions (85.06%) contained this 133-bp insertion (Fig. 6B). These results indicate that the frequency of the 133-bp insertion increased substantially due to selection during maize domestication. A phylogenetic tree was then constructed based on the 3.2-kb sequences from all 69 accessions, including four teosinte out groups, nine teosinte accessions, and 56 maize accessions (Dataset S3). The phylogenetic tree revealed that five teosinte accessions, all carrying the 133-bp insertion, grouped with maize accessions carrying this insertion, indicating that the 133-bp inser tion was introgressed from maize into teosinte (Fig. 6C). In addi tion, 16 teosinte and 4 maize accessions contained heterozygous alleles at this insertion in the tru1 gene (Fig. 6B). These results indicate that gene flow occurred frequently at the HELD1 locus between teosinte and maize.

Discussion

Transposons Are One of the Most Active Factors Shaping Maize Domestication. Transposons, as one of the most active factors, play key roles in genome evolution, including genome expansion, deletion, rearrangement, novel gene formation, and gene copy variation (26). Approximately 85% of the maize genome consists of various types of transposons (27). An increasing number of studies have revealed that transposons are a key factor in maize domestication. A Hopscotch transposable element inserted into the distal regulatory region of tb1 contributes primarily to increased apical dominance, thereby repressing tiller outgrowth during maize domestication ( ). A transposon insertion in 9 the regulatory region of gt1 enhanced its transcription and induced fewer, larger ears (28). Transposon insertions in the intron and a transposon-induced gene fusion occurred in two YABBY2 homologous genes on chromosomes 1 and 5, causing reduced disarticulation during maize domestication (29). Two transposons, inserted in the regulatory region and intron, respectively, reduced the expression of ZmCCT and resulted in early flowering during maize adaptation (30, 31). In pod corn, a transposon-derived sequence drives promoter rearrangement of a MADS-box gene, leading to gene duplication and ectopic expression in the female inflorescence (32, 33). As a result, thin glumes develop to cover all the kernels on the ear. Additionally, a transposable element was inserted into the CDS of C1 (34), resulting in the loss of anthocyanin pigments in maize. In this study, a 133-bp Harbinger transposable element was inserted into the intron of tru1 in maize during domestication. TB1 was recruited to bind the two sites in this 133-bp transposable element, which in turn enhances the transcriptional levels of tru1 and represses the extension of the husk leaf blade. The regulatory network connecting tb1 and tru1 appears to have formed rapidly during maize domestication. Transposons carrying abundant regulatory elements that jump throughout the genome would represent a major source of gene regulatory network evolution. All these findings demonstrate that transposable elements function as a key active factor shaping maize during domestication.

In this study, we identified a major QTL, HELD1, corresponding to tru1, that regulates husk leaf blade extension. A natural variant of 133-bp transposon insertion in the tru1 gene results in reduced husk leaf blade extension during maize domestication. Both the natural allele and knockout alleles showed that tru1 controls the ligular-auricular position not only in the husk leaf but also in the stem leaf, thereby enhancing the blade-to-sheath (husk) ratio and the leaf blade length. This finding is consistent with tru1 transcript and protein accumulation in the husk, stem leaf sheath, and ligule (1, 14).
